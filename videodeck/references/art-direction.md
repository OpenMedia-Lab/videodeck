# Art direction and motion

## Establish a visual system

Read the user's full references and identify concrete techniques with timestamps:
object construction, spatial layers, focus, transformations and handoffs. Compare
like-for-like actions at the intended viewing size. Use references as a craft
target, rather than copying their surface colors or assuming their every frame
is free of defects.

Keep a compact design brief shared across the series:

| Decision | What to specify |
| --- | --- |
| Composition | Main object, focal hierarchy, reading order, typography, geometry and protected math/label regions |
| Space and material | Dimensionality, object construction, foreground/background, projection, light direction, surface and contact cues |
| Motion | Object identity, paths, timing/easing, camera behavior, focus handoff and readable settled states |
| Color | Restrained palette and stable semantic roles; contrast across materials and depth |

A minimal palette can support richly constructed objects and motion. When depth
is requested, model meaningful faces, layered assemblies, thickness, lighting or
parallax; adding a drop shadow to every flat card is insufficient. Use tonal
gradients, edge highlights and shadows coherently within the palette. Keep math
legible and choose dimensionality per object; an equation need not be extruded.
Do not let perspective, area or thickness imply a false quantitative relationship.

## Prove the direction

Code representative static frames: opening/context, densest mechanism/formula,
and changed or closing state. Render real math, labels and materials; inspect at
phone size. Show an independently designed cover at thumbnail size as well.
Obtain the user's selection of a new direction through the interactive tool.

For a first or materially changed motion language, also show an actual encoded
sample before full production. Include a demanding mechanism and complete
transition: entry, transformation, focus handoff, settling and readable hold.
Use real narration timing when present. Static approval establishes layout and
color; it does not establish camera, depth, effects or motion quality. Reuse
accepted motion language for routine revisions and recheck affected intervals.

## Compose and animate the mechanism

Give each beat one dominant action. Preserve object identity as quantities move
or change representation; use the camera to explain scale, location and structure.
For example, a token's values can become a layer stack, compact into a cache block
and persist while a new block is appended. The transformation explains storage
scope; the color and token label keep the relationship traceable.

Shape acceleration, deceleration, stagger, secondary response and pauses around
the explanation. A data transfer can trace a path, an active component can scan,
and a result can settle before the next action. Vary the rhythm deliberately;
uniform fades/slides or an unmoving diagram throughout the narration are weak
substitutes for depicting the computation. Effects support the focal event and
leave necessary labels, formulas and results readable. Use overshoot, particles
or glow only where their behavior has a purpose; no effect is compulsory.

Use large primary objects and enough empty space to follow them. Remove
unnecessary microtext. Keep consistent type, line weights, materials and semantic
colors across shots; each scene still needs its own explanatory composition.

Record the intended content centerline and left/right insets. For a centered
portrait composition, compare the main diagram, equation, caption and cover
containers with the actual frame midpoint; symmetric player bars do not prove
the content is centered. Reserve interface clearance symmetrically when possible.
An asymmetric safe area changes the visual center and needs an explicit design
choice. Check the rendered pixels as well as CSS coordinates, including the
densest shot and closing card; left-aligned headings can remain left-aligned
inside a centered content region.

## Design occlusion explicitly

Intentional occlusion has a subject, layer order, time range and explanatory
purpose. Define protected text/formula regions and a readable hold for the key
state. Check entry, maximum overlap and exit: a traveling object through an
unrelated label, two competing titles or a result immediately covered by an
effect is a defect. Layout-validator exceptions describe specific planned
interactions; blanket overlap exemptions do not establish visual quality.

Palette, spatial treatment, narrator and motion references are adopted project
choices. The bundled technical example demonstrates runtime contracts; it is not
the production craft ceiling or a requirement for flat diagrams.
