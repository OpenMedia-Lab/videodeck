# Art direction and motion

Choose visual grammar from the subject, intended audience and references. Share
fonts, palette, captions and transition utilities across a series; compose each
explanation around its own mechanism. Leave room for design invention.

Code representative static frames first: opening/context, densest mechanism or
formula, and a changed state. Show the actual images at intended viewing size.
Use the host's interactive question tool for the user to select or adjust layout,
typography and palette before full animation; wait for the actual answer.
For a new ambiguous motion style, add a short representative motion sample. Already
accepted directions are reusable; every shot does not require a fresh approval.

Use large primary objects and enough empty space to follow them. Remove gratuitous
microtext; preserve necessary equations, captions and labels. Render formulas and
symbols before capture; raw LaTeX or a tiny illegible equation is not a substitute.
Check margins and captions at phone size, including platform interface safe areas.

Design transitions as part of the explanation: an object travels into the next
context, a camera follows the same quantity, a scan reveals structure, or particles
reconstruct a meaningful state. Choose among these; no effect is mandatory.
Motion direction, color and object identity must match the causal relationship.
Check both the fast midpoint and settled endpoints. Avoid transition overlap hiding
the very state the narration is explaining.

Design a separate cover with the real topic and one strong visual idea. Inspect
it at thumbnail size; it should remain legible and represent this video's content.
A random exported frame is not an independent cover design.

An opt-in series direction can use black/white, neutral gray and one accent color,
plus a calm low male narrator. Specific blue values, frame rates, padding counts,
shot counts, length limits and model vendors are project choices, not universal
requirements. “No music in the last video” does not establish a permanent ban.
