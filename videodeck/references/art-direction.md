# Art direction and motion

## Establish the design

Read the user's complete references. Identify concrete techniques with timestamps:
construction, layers, focus, transformations and handoffs. Compare like-for-like
actions at intended viewing size; a reference's defects are not a quality target.
Keep one compact design brief across the series:

| Decision | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Composition | Main object, hierarchy, typography, geometry, protected text/math regions |
| Construction | 2D or scoped 3D, object structure, layers and any material/light cues |
| Motion | Object identity, paths, timing/easing, focus handoffs and readable holds |
| Color | Restrained palette, stable semantic roles and legible contrast |

## Choose dimensionality

Start with 2D. Choose the least complex representation that explains the mechanism
clearly, considering authoring, rendering and revision cost. Use 3D only when a
spatial relation, physical arrangement, occlusion or viewpoint is necessary to
the explanation and 2D cannot show it clearly; record its reason and scene/object
scope. GPU component placement may justify a spatial view; GPU scheduling may
remain 2D.
A hardware topic alone is not a reason for 3D. If 2D preserves the necessary
relationship clearly, use it. Prefer local 3D when sufficient.

MUST NOT extrude every token, matrix, label or equation to signal polish or satisfy
“depth.” Dimensionality is not a quality ranking. A minimal palette still requires
precise construction, hierarchy, alignment, layering, paths, timing and readable
holds. Effects are optional. Spatial objects require coherent projection, light,
edges, contact and occlusion; perspective or thickness must not imply false data.

## Prove the direction

Code representative opening, densest mechanism/formula and closing states. Render
real notation and labels; inspect at phone size. Design an independent cover and
inspect its thumbnail. Obtain the user's selection of a new direction through the
interactive tool; reuse an accepted direction without repeating that choice.

For a first or materially changed motion language, also show an actual encoded
sample before full production. Include a demanding mechanism and complete
transition: entry, transformation, focus handoff, settling and readable hold,
using actual narration timing when present. Static approval does not approve
motion, depth or effects.

## Compose the mechanism

Give each beat one dominant action. Preserve object identity as quantities move or
change representation. Use camera motion only to explain location, scale or
structure; 2D position, grouping and layering can express these relationships.

Design acceleration, deceleration, stagger, secondary response and pauses around
the explanation. A transfer can trace a path and a result can settle before the
next action. Uniform fades/slides or an unmoving diagram do not substitute for
showing computation. Effects must support the focal event and leave required
labels, formulas and results readable. Keep typography, line weights and semantic
colors consistent; compose each scene for its own explanation.

Record the intended content centerline and left/right insets. Compare diagram,
equation, caption and cover containers with the actual frame midpoint, including
dense and closing states. Symmetric player bars do not prove centered content.
Prefer symmetric interface clearance; an asymmetric safe area needs an explicit
design choice. Left-aligned headings may remain inside a centered content region.

## Preserve transitions

Save the accepted encoded sample and a compact motion specification: object
correspondence, paths, timing/easing, stagger, camera/focus handoff, overlap and
settled states. These are revision constraints, not just style adjectives.
A label, palette or math repair MUST NOT discard or simplify accepted transitions.
If a requested change conflicts with them, identify the exact conflict before
replacing approved motion; preserve settled choices elsewhere.

Design each boundary as outgoing state → transition → incoming state. For a
continuous passage, carry the object or relationship through it with coherent
position, scale, direction and velocity, then hand off focus and settle. A scene
endpoint MUST NOT silently clip exits or replace accepted fluid motion with hard
cuts, resets or generic fades. Intentional cuts need a narrative purpose consistent
with the accepted direction; not every boundary needs a morph.

## Control occlusion

Intentional occlusion requires a subject, layer order, time range and explanatory
purpose. Protect text/math and the key state's readable hold. Check entry, maximum
overlap and exit, including actor thickness and shadows. Traveling through an
unrelated label, competing titles or covering a result is a defect. Validator
exceptions must identify specific planned interactions; blanket exemptions prove
nothing. The bundled example demonstrates runtime contracts, not a production style.
